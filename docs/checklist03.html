Overview¶

Here is a general outline of the key concepts (arranged by topic) that you should know for Exam 03.

The exam will have the following format:

  1. Files, Directories, I/O: (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, free response).

  2. Sockets, HTTP: (multiple-choice, fill-in-the-blank, free response).

  3. Walking: (coding on computer).

Parts 1 through 2 are to be done first on paper. Once that portion is completed and submitted, you will do part 3 on your own computer and submit code to your assignments repository.

Note: REST and Vial will not be on Exam 03, but you should have completed and be familiar with the playlist_server.py in Homework 09.

Representative, But Not Exhaustive¶

This checklist is meant to be representative, rather than exhaustive (ie. there may be questions that show up on the exam that are not shown below).

Logistics¶

Exam 03 will take place during our normal lecture session on Wednesday, November 18 from 11:30 AM - 12:20 PM in 303 Cushing Hall.

As noted above, the first portion of the exam with be on paper, while the remaining component with require coding and submitting work to your assignments GitHub repository.

During the paper portion of the exam, students will only be allowed to use one cheatsheet. This paper component of the exam must be submitted before students can access the coding section.

For the coding portion, students will use their own computer, and thus are permitted access to any material in their textbooks, notes, assignments, and the Internet.

Honor Code Violations¶

Although students are allowed to use their computers and the Internet during the coding portion of the exam, students may not communicate with others or solicit answers from others. Nor may they use AI tools such as ChatGPT or Co-Pilot during the exam. Students caught sharing solutions or violating any portion of the honor code will receive a zero on the exam.

Furthermore, students are not allowed to consult, copy, or share material from previous sections of this course.

System Calls¶

  1. What exactly are system calls? How are they different from normal functions?

  2. What are some reasons why system calls related to files, directories, i/o, and sockets would fail?

  3. How could these system calls fail?

  4. How do we check or detect these failures?

Sample Questions¶

  1. You are unsure why a program is failing. How would you check what system calls it is using?

Files, Directories, I/O¶

  1. What is the difference between open and os.open?

  2. What exactly is a file descriptor and what system calls can we do with one?

  3. How do we get the inode information for a file? What are the common attributes in a stat structure?

Sample Questions¶

How would you use system calls in Python to accomplish the following tasks:

  1. Display the contents of a file?

  2. Copy the contents of a file?

  3. Check if a file is a regular file or directory?

  4. Check if a file is a readable, writable, or executable?

  5. Check the size of the file?

  6. List the contents of a directory?

  7. Access a particular byte with in a file?

  8. Determine the owner of a file?

OOP¶

  1. How do we implement and use the context manger protocol?

  2. How do we implement and use the factory design pattern?

Sample Questions¶

  1. Describe how and why the factory design pattern was used in Homework 07.

  2. Describe how and why the context manager protocol was used in Homework 08.

Sockets, HTTP¶

  1. What is a URL and what are its components?

  2. How are sockets similar to files? How are they different?

  3. What is HTTP?

    • What system calls does a typical HTTP client perform? What messages does it send and receive?

    • What system calls does a typical HTTP server perform? What messages does it send and receive?
